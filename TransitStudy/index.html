Tahoe Transit Study

The Tahoe Regional Planning Agency (TRPA) is leading a regional effort to create a more coordinated, efficient, and sustainable transit system throughout the Lake Tahoe Basin. The draft study is now available for review and input.

Read the reports Take the survey

What is the Tahoe Transit Study?

The Tahoe Transit Study is a comprehensive operational analysis (COA) that builds upon recently completed short- and long-range transit plans to provide a blueprint for transit agencies to improve service, align governance, and secure sustainable funding.

Project objectives:

  1. Optimize service: Develop short- and long-term service plans with a focus on connectivity.
  2. Strengthen governance: Clarify roles and responsibilities and learn from other areas.
  3. Leverage funding: Assess funding structures and agreements and stretch dollars.

    Questions

    Frequently asked questions

    What transportation services are already operated in the Tahoe Basin?

    The study takes into consideration:

    • Tahoe Truckee Area Regional Transit (TART)
    • TART Connect
    • Tahoe Transportation District (TTD)
    • Lake Link
    • Airport services
    • Seasonal shuttle services
    What is TRPA's role?

    TRPA, acting as the metropolitan planning organization (MPO) and the regional transportation planning agency (RTPA) for the Lake Tahoe Basin, is responsible for maintaining the Regional Transportation Plan and coordinating with transit operators to update short- and long-range transit plans. TRPA is also responsible for administering and allocating a large majority of state and federal transit funding, including the California Transportation Development Act and dedicated formula Federal Transit Administration funds.

    Who are the project partners?

    TRPA is leading the study in coordination with the Transit Working Group, which includes representatives from TRPA, TTD, the North and South Shore TMAs, and every local jurisdiction, including the City of South Lake Tahoe, El Dorado County, Douglas County, Washoe County, Placer County, and Carson City.

    How is this project different from the operators' Short Range Transit Plans and System Plan Updates or TRPA's Regional Transportation Plan?

    TTD's Short Range Transit Plan and TART's System Plan Update evaluated each of their respective operations specifically. This planning effort will consider both public and private operators for the entire Tahoe Basin at a higher level, building upon the analyses already conducted by TTD and TART.

    TRPA's Regional Transportation Plan includes a long-term vision for transit services over the next 25 years, outlining a layered build-out of transit services and the funding gap. The Regional Transportation Plan does not define who will operate which services or how the expanded services will be funded, issues that the COA will help address.

    Who oversees transit in the basin today

    Five public boards direct and oversee transit operations in the Tahoe Basin. Their members represent local jurisdictions, state and regional agencies, community members, and the nonprofit and private sectors. Private operators run their own services.

          Lake Tahoe Transit Working Group

          The Lake Tahoe Transit Working Group is an informal collaborative body dedicated to addressing local and regional transit issues within the Lake Tahoe Basin. The Transit Working Group meets monthly and includes staff-level representatives from each of the local jurisdictions, the Tahoe Transportation District, Tahoe Regional Planning Agency, and the transportation management associations.
