Task 4: Funding and implementation strategy

How transit in the Tahoe Region is paid for, route by route

This model brings the region's transit operating costs and funding sources into one place. It shows how each source is applied to each route, checks those uses against each source's eligibility rules and match requirements, and projects the balance five years out. It covers the existing funding inventory (Task 4a), the dynamic funding model (Task 4b), and a starting inventory of funding agreements (Task 4c). Every number is traced to the workbook cell or fact sheet it came from; anything you change is logged and marked.

  1. Routes and costs: check service hours, cost rates, and which jurisdictions each route serves.
  2. Funding sources: review amounts, match requirements, and restrictions. Click Details on any source to edit it.
  3. Allocation and rules: set the share of each source applied to each route. Cells turn red when a rule is broken.
  4. Results, Five-year outlook, Agreements and risk: read the balance by route, agency, and jurisdiction, and see which funds are time-limited or uncommitted.

        FTA 5307 sub-allocation between TTD and Placer County

        The Lake Tahoe urbanized area (UZA) apportionment is split by an earned-share formula: the average of each operator's share of urban revenue miles, population, and ridership. 75 percent of 5307 goes to operations. The FTA 5307 formula rows for TTD and TART use this result unless you unlink them in Details.

        Source: Financial PlanG › FTA Allocation Split (26-27) (FY 27 values are the FY 25-26 apportionment plus 1 percent, marked "EXAMPLE"). FTA directs the MPO and designated recipients to set the sub-area split through a "fair and rational" process (Revenue %s AD59).
